# Plain-Language Insurance Concepts

## 1. Sum Insured vs Sum Assured
- **Sum Insured (Health Insurance)**: The maximum amount the insurance company will reimburse or pay for your medical treatments during a policy year. Unused amounts do not carry over unless the policy features an automatic restore benefit.
- **Sum Assured (Life / Term Insurance)**: The guaranteed fixed payout given to your nominees upon the unfortunate death of the life assured.

## 2. Waiting Periods
- **Initial Waiting Period**: 30 days from the policy start date during which illnesses cannot be claimed. Accidents are covered immediately from Day 1.
- **Pre-Existing Disease (PED) Waiting Period**: Medical conditions that you already had before purchasing the policy (e.g., diabetes, hypertension, asthma) are covered after a continuous waiting period (typically 24 to 36 months).
- **Specific Illness Waiting Period**: Procedures like hernia, joint replacements, cataract, or kidney stones often have a 24-month waiting duration.

## 3. Co-Payment & Deductibles
- **Co-Payment**: A clause where the policyholder agrees to pay a pre-fixed percentage (e.g., 10% or 20%) of the total approved hospital claim, and the insurer pays the remaining balance. Opting for a co-pay lowers your annual premium.
- **Deductible**: A flat amount you must pay out-of-pocket before insurance coverage kicks in.

## 4. Cashless Hospitalization vs Reimbursement
- **Cashless Treatment**: Available at empaneled network hospitals. The Third-Party Administrator (TPA) or insurer settles hospital bills directly with the medical center.
- **Reimbursement**: If treated at a non-network hospital, you pay the bills upon discharge and submit original bills, discharge summary, and pharmacy receipts for post-treatment reimbursement.

## 5. Claim Settlement Ratio (CSR)
The percentage of total claims received by an insurance company that it successfully pays out during a financial year. A CSR above 95% indicates reliable claims processing.
